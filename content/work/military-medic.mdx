## The premise

A medic in a combat zone has no connectivity and no free hands. The agent listens, asks the triage questions in order, and speaks the protocol back — tourniquet placement, dose by weight, when to move — with nothing leaving the device.

## What made it hard

Everything on-device means a small model, a small speech stack, and a strict latency budget on a phone. The work was in the tool schema: a handful of functions (vitals, injury classification, protocol lookup, timers) with tight enough descriptions that a 4-billion-parameter model calls the right one, and a conversation loop that tolerates being interrupted.

## Why I flew for it

The hackathon was at Y Combinator's office, run by a YC company with DeepMind. I wanted to see the room. The prize was a guaranteed YC interview; I didn't win it, and the trip was worth it anyway.
