## The plan

Homer only — no likeness of anyone else's film. Twelve chapters, eight spoken moments, one melody (the sirens') that returns at the end. Cast sheets locked, a style anchor locked after three rounds of "too dark, too murky" turned out to be an upstream reference problem rather than per-shot lighting. Three render tiers: Seedance 2.5 at 1080p for hero shots, LTX-2.3 on my own GPU for free B-roll, stills with parallax for the rest.

## What the first live session taught me

- The economics, exactly: a prepaid token package works out to $7.04 per million tokens, and a token count is `width × height × 24 fps × seconds ÷ 1024` plus a fixed overhead. A ten-second 1080p shot is $3.44. Nine hundred seconds of hero footage fits inside the cap.
- Shoot one ten-second shot per generation. Three tens cost the same as one thirty and a retry costs a third.
- Three moderation gates. The input filter rejects every 2×4 storyboard grid — eight bordered faces look like a photo contact sheet to it. The output filter says "copyright" when it means "sensitive content." And never *negate* a sensitive word; "no kissing" still ships the token.
- A reference image is a hint; a first-frame image is a fact. Identity drifted on everything shot with references.

Footage on disk: two shots of Circe. That's it.

## What changes

Every comparable channel says don't ship a seventy-minute single drop first. The ones that held their audience shipped cheap short prologues before the expensive chapters. So: Cyclops, Sirens, Calypso, Circe as standalone pieces, a two-minute sirens music video, then the feature if the prologues earn it. The promotional pricing ends September 17; the plan is to spend it on things that ship as uploads.
